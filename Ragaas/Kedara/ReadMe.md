
Kedara and Kedaragoula are same?

No, Kedara and Kedaragoula (also spelled Kedaragaula or Kedaragowla) are distinct ragas in the Carnatic music tradition.

While their names sound similar, they differ significantly in their structure, scale (swaras), and musical character:

Kedara (or Kedaram): This is a janya raga derived from the 29th Melakarta, Dheerasankarabharanam. It is known for its vibrant and majestic quality.
Kedaragoula: This is a janya raga derived from the 28th Melakarta, Harikambhoji. It is an audava-sampoorna raga (five notes in ascent, seven in descent) and is often associated with a serene, devotional, and flowing quality.

Because they originate from different parent scales and possess unique grammatical frameworks, they are considered separate melodic entities by musicians and scholars. Confusing the two is a common point of discussion for students of Carnatic music, as distinguishing them often requires listening to their specific phrasing and prayogas (musical patterns) rather than relying on notation alone.
