



Devagandhari is derived from Sankarabharanam

In Carnatic music, Devagandhari is classified as a janya raga (derived scale), and its melakarta (parent scale or janaka raga) is Dheerasankarabharanam (the 29th Melakarta)

Key Characteristics & DistinctionWhile it shares its foundational note structure with Sankarabharanam, Devagandhari is unique in how it is handled:

The Scale: It is an audava-vakra-sampurna ragam. Its scale typically skips certain notes in the ascent but uses them with specific zig-zag (vakra) patterns and microtones (gamakas) in the descent.

The Arabhi Connection: Devagandhari uses nearly identical notes to another famous Sankarabharanam derivative, Arabhi. However, Arabhi is sung briskly and evokes valour (veera rasa), while Devagandhari is sung much slower, emphasizing intense devotion, peace, and compassion (karuna/shanta rasa).

A Bhashanga Touch: In some traditional frameworks, Devagandhari is considered a bhashanga raga because it occasionally introduces a foreign note—the Kaisika Nishadam—outside of Sankarabharanam's strict Kakali Nishadam.

Note: Do not confuse this with Karnataka Devagandhari, which is an entirely different raga derived from Kharaharapriya (similar to modern Abheri)
