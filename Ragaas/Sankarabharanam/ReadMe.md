Smt. Bombay Jayashri - Annai Janaki - Shankarabharanam - Arunachala Kavirayar


The kriti "Annai Janaki Vandale" (composed by Arunachala Kavirayar from his Rama Natakam) 
is traditionally and predominantly rendered in the Saveri ragam. 
However, some modern or alternate renditions (such as those by certain vocalists) 
have occasionally set or explored it in Sankarabharanam


Artist RenderingsThe two versions of this song differ distinctly in melody, 
rhythm, and performing artists:The Saveri Version (Adi Talam): 
This is considered the traditional rendering. 
It is performed by prominent vocalists like Sanjay Subrahmanyam, Neyveli R. Santhanagopalan, Lakshmi Rangarajan, 
and Geetha Raja.
The Sankarabharanam Version (Misra Chapu or Adi Talam): This alternate version brings a bright, majestic quality to the scene. 
It is famously associated with the legendary maestro K. V. Narayanaswamy (singing in Misra Chapu) 
and has been beautifully rendered by contemporary maestros like Bombay Jayashri (set to Adi Talam) 
and the lineage of D. K. Pattammal

Technical Details & MeaningComposer: Arunachala Kavirayar (18th Century).
Context: This song is from the Bala Kandam section of his masterpiece opera, the Rama Natakam. 
It vividly sets the stage for the divine wedding (Sita Kalyanam) as Mother Sita arrives at the marriage hall.
Summary of the MeaningThe song is a highly visual, poetic celebration of Sita Devi’s grace as she enters her father King Janaka's jewelled marriage hall.
Pallavi & Anupallavi: "Mother Janaki has arrived! She grants her divine presence to all the kings of kings." 
The poet describes her walking into the assembly surrounded by her young maidens (sakhi-s). 
She looks like the moon shining brightly in the middle of a cluster of stars, carrying the radiant glow of gold and the sweet fragrance of fresh flowers.
Charanam Highlights: The lyrical beauty reaches its peak in the description of her gait. 
As she takes her beautiful steps, the light reflecting from her gem-studded anklets (silambu) onto the floor makes it look as though Mother Earth (Bhooma Devi) has personally laid out a soft, glowing carpet of deer skin for her daughter. 
Furthermore, out of pure shyness and modesty, Sita does not lift her head to look directly at Lord Rama. 
Instead, as she holds up the wedding garland, she catches a beautiful glimpse of Rama's reflection mirrored in her own shining bangles
